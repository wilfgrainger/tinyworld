## Five-minute route

1. Spawn and look around without explanation for 30 seconds.
2. Ask the player to say what they think the game is.
3. Let them choose where to go first.
4. Let them find or claim a plot and inspect the home.
5. Offer the daily loop only if they cannot discover it:
   - fountain reward;
   - garden;
   - Courier parcel;
   - Village Shop delivery.
6. Let them find the Tiny Bike and describe what they expect it to do.
7. Let them walk toward the woods, cliffs, or sea.
8. Point out the portal only if they miss it, then ask what they expect inside.
9. With two or more players, attempt a visit or Trading Post exchange.
10. Ask whether they would play again tomorrow and what they would want next.

Do not coach the answer before recording the first response.

## First-impression questions

- What is this game about?
- Where would you go first?
- What looks most interesting?
- What looks unfinished or confusing?
- Which place feels like the centre of the village?
- Can you tell which plot could become yours?

## Functional questions

- Was the daily loop understandable?
- Did the fountain, garden, job, and shop feel connected?
- Did the Tiny Bike look and feel like a bike?
- Did the home feel personal or merely assigned?
- Did the portal create curiosity?
- Did progression make you want to improve the home or character?
- Did anything feel like work without a satisfying result?

## Social and safety questions

- Could you tell what was public and what was private?
- Did visiting another home feel understandable and safe?
- Did the Trading Post confirmation feel clear?
- Did you know what another player could and could not change?
- Did any sign, prompt, or UI element feel pressuring?

## Stop conditions

Stop and preserve evidence if any of these occur:

- red runtime error;
- lost or duplicated profile progress;
- duplicate reward;
- prompt cannot be reached or is obstructed;
- player is trapped by decoration;
- grass, border, water, or new geometry flickers severely;
- session conflict can overwrite active progress;
- scale budget reports over-budget;
- a participant feels unsafe, pressured, or unable to leave the social interaction.
