# TinyWorld v0.0.2 alpha family test

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Branch:** `feat/v0.0.2-alpha`  
**Status:** visual candidate for controlled family testing; not a public release

This guide tests whether TinyWorld feels like the proposal: a cosy life in a small village, a clear daily job loop, visible home and transport progression, friendly social spaces, and an exciting portal into an impossible world. It preserves the v0.01 gameplay contract while checking the v0.0.2 presentation pass.

The reference bar is the supplied TinyWorld proposal imagery: colourful voxel-like homes and gardens, readable dark story cards, a visible daily loop, strong house identity, travel, trade, and a spectacular Giant Kitchen reveal. The goal is not to copy another game or add asset-heavy production art. The goal is for a child to understand the world, choose a next action, and want to return.

## Before testing

- Use the Rojo-synced `TinyWorld Dev` experience, not a production place.
- Keep the experience limited to named playtesters. The [remote family playtest guide](remote-playtest.md) covers access and device setup.
- For a first-run test, use a profile that has not completed onboarding. For persistence, use a separate completed profile.
- Keep the Studio Output pane visible during local testing. Stop if a red runtime error appears.
- Record the tester's device and input method, but do not record passwords, account identifiers, or private chat.

## Gameplay regression route

Run this after the visual checks. The expected rewards and authority must remain the v0.01 contract.

1. Complete first-run onboarding with a valid name, Boy/Girl avatar style, and free Meadow/Harbor/Sunset outfit. Confirm invalid names are rejected and a valid setup saves.
2. Stop and rejoin. Confirm the welcome panel is skipped and the identity persists.
3. Claim the fountain reward once. Confirm the second claim on the same UTC day is refused.
4. Plant, water, wait, and harvest a carrot. Confirm the inventory and XP update.
5. Take a Courier parcel, deliver it, and confirm the parcel clears and the server rewards the player.
6. Buy/toggle the Tiny Bike when eligible. Confirm ownership and active/parked state persist.
7. Upgrade the home when both level and coin requirements are met. Confirm the physical tier rebuild and the HUD goal changes to the next valid action.
8. Cycle plot privacy through Open, Friends, Private, and Open. With a second player, confirm access follows the setting.
9. Run a two-player trade. Confirm offers are real inventory items, both players confirm, and changing an offer clears confirmation.
10. Enter the Giant Kitchen, collect all three Sugar Crystals, return through the portal, and confirm the permanent reward and completion count.
11. Check Studio Output after the route. Any red error is a failed acceptance item even if the visible interaction appeared to work.
12. Repeat the HUD check in a phone-sized Studio emulator or on a real permitted playtest device. Record clipping, unreadable text, blocked prompts, or uncomfortable camera movement.

## Core-loop and future day-job guardrails

TinyWorld is intended to become a safe, durable creator-led opportunity for the young people building it and a welcoming life sandbox for the children testing it. The core loop should therefore create lasting value through returnable work, visible mastery, creative ownership, social connection, and new worlds to discover.

The v0.0.2 alpha deliberately contains:

- no Robux purchase prompts;
- no fake game-pass or developer-product IDs;
- no MarketplaceService purchase code;
- no immersive ads;
- no Premium upsell; and
- only an informational Premium Preview sign stating that future options must be cosmetic, expressive, or bounded convenience.

Future monetization may be considered only after a separate product, safety, and QA review. The initial candidate categories are:

- cosmetic identity and outfit variations;
- home decoration, garden styling, furniture, and expressive effects;
- private or creative social-space options;
- bounded convenience that saves time without increasing gameplay power; and
- creator-supporting seasonal or showcase content that does not gate the core life loop.

Robux must not buy levels, coins, XP, rare resources, house tiers, bike speed, portal completion, trade advantages, or access to the basic village/job/adventure loop. A free player must retain equivalent gameplay power. Server validation remains authoritative for every economy and progression change.

Before implementing any live product, use real IDs created in the Creator Dashboard, document the product contract, test cancellation/retry/failure states, and repeat this family-safety review. Roblox's official [passes guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/passes), [developer products guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/developer-products), [Creator Rewards documentation](https://create.roblox.com/docs/creator-rewards), and [DevEx/monetization overview](https://create.roblox.com/docs/get-started/monetization) are the sources to re-check because eligibility and platform terms can change.

## Feedback format

For a failed or confusing step, record:

- tester nickname only;
- device and input method;
- the numbered step;
- what the tester expected;
- what actually happened;
- the visible message or screenshot; and
- Studio Output text if reproducing locally.

Do not turn a single child's confusion into a monetization prompt. First improve clarity, pacing, or world feedback and retest the loop.
