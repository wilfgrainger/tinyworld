# Experience pillars

Each pillar names an observable player behaviour, a failure condition and an evidence method. Content count alone does not satisfy a pillar.

## Build your life

**Promise:** the home is a playable expression of progress, not a menu backdrop.

Observable behaviour:

- player enters recognisable rooms;
- uses physical furniture/appliances;
- buys/owns/places/stores items;
- sees saved changes after rejoin;
- changes appearance through a wardrobe/home-life surface.

Failure: decorating is menu-only, placed objects are anonymous geometry, or persistent changes do not survive safely.

Evidence: first 10/30-minute playtest, save/rejoin route, labels-off home-recognition route.

## Belong to a village

**Promise:** the village is small enough to learn and varied enough to remember.

Observable behaviour:

- player can navigate among distinct civic destinations/neighbourhoods;
- completes ordinary-life activities without portal dependency;
- encounters bounded ambient life;
- visits another home with predictable privacy rules.

Failure: the village feels like a lobby or requires waypoint/label decoding for every destination.

Evidence: unprompted navigation/playtest observations, two-client privacy/visit route.

## Explore impossible worlds

**Promise:** portals deliver authored contrast and feed ordinary life.

Observable behaviour:

- each world has an obvious visual rule;
- player engages a world-specific traversal/activity mechanic;
- discovers a secret or optional detail;
- completes a bounded objective;
- returns with a keepsake/resource/home payoff.

Failure: a portal is only a reskinned collection room or creates a disconnected progression game.

Evidence: Studio route for all four worlds plus return-home payoff observation.

## Discover secrets

**Promise:** attention to the environment produces gentle surprise.

Observable behaviour:

- player notices landmarks/environmental clues;
- can find optional collectibles or hidden interactions;
- builds a meaningful collection rather than raw counters.

Failure: discoveries exist only as UI notifications or unexplained telemetry.

Evidence: uncoached playtest notes and collection/places journal state.

## Feel safe and respected

**Promise:** TinyWorld protects player trust and avoids coercive design.

Observable behaviour:

- server rejects hostile price/reward/ownership/placement/trade input;
- save failures fail closed;
- home privacy behaves predictably;
- free character identity remains meaningful;
- purchase-like UX is reserved for actual ownership transactions.

Failure: the client can mint state, inaccessible saves are replaced, social permissions are ambiguous or gameplay power is monetised.

Evidence: automated validator tests, hostile remote route, multi-client privacy/trade route and family playtest.

## Feel good in the hands

**Promise:** core play is readable and tactile on touch, mouse and controller.

Observable behaviour:

- >=44x44 effective touch targets;
- no hover-only critical action;
- deliberate controller focus;
- physical interactions acknowledge input through object state/motion/light/sound/short feedback;
- ordinary phone play remains readable and performant.

Failure: clipped UI, controller traps, per-frame network spam, or interaction feedback exists only in text.

Evidence: phone/controller device matrix plus performance route.

## Feature test

A feature may ship only when it strengthens at least one pillar without weakening server authority, recognisability, mobile usability, safety or performance budgets. Features that merely increase counters, telemetry or database surface do not meet the bar.
