# TinyWorld v0.0.4 MVP Design

## Goal

Raise the current v0.0.3 living-village slice into a visually readable, multiplayer-testable MVP that feels like the supplied TinyWorld proposal: a child can see a cosy home, a shared village life loop, roads and transport, a social economy, an impossible portal world, and a deliberate woods/cliffs/sea boundary.

The product north star remains:

> **Build your life. Explore impossible worlds. Discover the secrets of TinyWorld.**

The longer-term ambition is a safe, durable creator-led opportunity that could become meaningful work for the young people building the game. v0.0.4 therefore protects free access to the core life loop and does not introduce pay-to-win mechanics, fake products, purchase prompts, or ads.

## Evidence and current gap

The v0.0.3 code already proves the main contracts: persistent profiles, onboarding, a daily reward, gardening, Courier work, home upgrades, Tiny Bike transport, portal progression, a two-sided trade state machine, a civic contribution, and a capacity-aware woods/cliffs/sea perimeter. The current live screenshots and proposal references expose the next gap:

- the world reads as a prototype plane with isolated signboards rather than a lived-in village;
- the central plaza and street hierarchy are not strong enough to guide a first-time player;
- plot borders and the legacy Studio Baseplate have shared surface heights that can shimmer during movement;
- houses have the right tier contract but need stronger gardens, fences, entrances, and visual ownership cues;
- multiplayer geometry and social spaces need a deliberate Server & Clients acceptance route;
- the HUD shows state but does not yet present the daily loop as a compact, child-readable story.

## Scope

### Arrival and civic core

The generated village gets one clear arrival square around the fountain, with a readable axis to the Town Hall, Village Shop, Courier Depot, Transport Shop, Trading Post, and portal plaza. The core uses a small, deterministic set of primitives:

- asphalt or concrete road lanes with stone kerbs;
- a fountain/paved square with lanterns, benches, flower beds, and market stalls;
- landmark signs placed at building entrances rather than floating in the main camera path;
- unobstructed prompt zones and one visually obvious next-action route.

All scenery is generated by code and remains bounded by the configured player capacity. No Creator Store models or manual Studio source edits are part of the release.

### Streets, plots, and homes

The four original plot slots remain stable. Additional slots continue to come from the deterministic Players.MaxPlayers layout contract. Each plot receives:

- one authoritative PlotBase top surface;
- a separated, non-collidable stone/hedge border with an explicit vertical offset;
- a visible gate/approach connection to the village road;
- a small garden, tree, flowers, and an owner/home sign;
- the existing server-owned kiosks and privacy contract.

House tiers keep their existing upgrade rules. The visual rebuild adds stronger porch, roof, window, garden, and boundary readability without changing prices, progression, or ownership authority.

### Boundary

The village edge becomes a readable transition rather than an abrupt square:

village ground -> road/clearing -> woodland or stepped cliff -> sand -> sea

The boundary remains one shared model per server, sized from the outermost plot ring. Woods are decorative and non-collidable, cliffs are anchored/collidable navigation cues, sand is a non-collidable transition, and sea is non-collidable water. The three existing Woodland Trail, Cliff Lookout, and Sea Dock landmarks remain the daily exploration route.

### Multiplayer MVP

The release must be playable with more than one player in the same server:

- separate players receive separate plots from the server-owned assignment table;
- private/friends/open home access remains server-authoritative;
- the Trading Post supports the existing two-player join/offer/confirm/exchange flow;
- the central roads and prompt zones remain usable when two avatars occupy the village;
- Studio Server & Clients is a required acceptance run, not a claim that live cross-server scale has been proven.

The layout test covers requested capacities 1, 4, 8, 16, 32, 50, and 60; the runtime gate uses the configured Studio server capacity available in the place.

### Player-facing clarity

The HUD becomes a compact v0.0.4 daily-life card. It retains the authoritative attributes already exposed by the server and adds only presentation:

- version/name and identity;
- coins, level/XP, home/privacy, inventory, profession, transport;
- portal and boundary progress;
- a short TODAY route that makes the reward -> work/garden -> improve -> explore/trade loop legible.

No client code is allowed to mint or validate coins, XP, ownership, prices, rewards, or trade contents.

## Architecture

The existing WorldBuilder remains the orchestration boundary. Visual responsibilities are narrowed into deterministic builders rather than adding another gameplay framework:

- src/shared/WorldLayoutRules.luau owns numeric plot, road, civic-core, boundary, and budget geometry. It remains Roblox-service-free.
- src/server/WorldBuilder.luau owns stable base surfaces, plot scaffolding, prompts, portal setup, and calls the dedicated scenery/boundary builders once per server.
- src/server/VillageSceneryBuilder.luau owns the civic square, streets, kerbs, market dressing, lanterns, and low-density woodland/flower dressing that belongs inside the boundary.
- src/server/BoundaryBuilder.luau owns the shared woods/cliffs/sand/sea perimeter and the three exploration landmarks.
- src/server/PlotService.luau owns server-authoritative house rebuilds, plot ownership labels, gates, and privacy/visit behavior.
- src/client/Main.client.luau owns only the v0.0.4 HUD presentation.

The builders use a small common convention: a walkable surface is a collidable Part with an explicitly owned top height; decorative overlays are non-collidable, non-touching, non-querying parts placed above or beside that surface. Tests and static guards assert the named broad surfaces and borders do not return to coplanar placement.

## Data flow and multiplayer safety

1. Main.server.luau reads Players.MaxPlayers and calls WorldLayoutRules.create(maxPlayers) through WorldBuilder.build(maxPlayers).
2. The world is built once, before player callbacks, and returned as a contract containing plots, prompts, portal references, trade references, and boundary references.
3. PlotService assigns the first available slot to each loaded player and releases it on PlayerRemoving.
4. Gameplay services read and mutate the server-owned profile, then call PlayerStateService.sync and ProfileStore.save using the existing patterns.
5. The client observes attributes and renders the HUD; it never decides whether a reward, exchange, upgrade, or plot visit is valid.

No new remote economy API is needed for this slice. That keeps the release surface small while making the shared world and the existing social mechanics visible enough to test.

## Error handling and safety

- If profile loading fails, the existing fail-closed kick behavior remains; no fresh replacement profile is created.
- If there are more players than generated plots, the player remains able to use public village activities and receives the existing explanatory message.
- If an owner leaves, the plot is cleared, its label/privacy reset, and its slot becomes available for the next player.
- If a trade profile changes or a player leaves, confirmations are invalidated and no partial exchange occurs.
- If a surface or scenery budget calculation is invalid, the pure layout rules fall back to safe minimums and the builder uses bounded counts.

## Verification and release gates

### Static/local gates

- all shared behavior specs pass;
- Luau analysis and server/client compilation pass;
- material/surface guard rejects broad Grass gameplay slabs, retained Baseplate surfaces, and coplanar plot borders;
- Rojo builds the exact default.project.json tree;
- git diff --check is clean.

### Studio gates

- fresh single-player Play loads the v0.0.4 HUD and no red Output errors;
- walking across the civic ground, a plot border, a house approach, and the boundary shows no movement shimmer;
- Server & Clients shows two players with separate plots and usable public routes;
- both players can complete the Trading Post confirmation flow without client-side authority;
- stop/rejoin restores identity, progression, inventory, home, route, and privacy state;
- the Rojo-synced current commit is published to the limited TinyWorld Dev experience.

The 48-hour target is a controlled MVP launch to named playtesters, not a claim of production-scale reliability, public discovery, live monetization, or a guaranteed cross-server player count.

## Deliberate non-goals for v0.0.4

- no live Robux products, monetization prompts, or ads;
- no asset-pack import pipeline;
- no free-form player building or decoration persistence;
- no cars, planes, boats, pets, elections, seasonal events, or multiple portal worlds beyond the existing Giant Kitchen proof;
- no claim that a local Server & Clients run proves remote-device, DataStore contention, or cross-server behavior.
